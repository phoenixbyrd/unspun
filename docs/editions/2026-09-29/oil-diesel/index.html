Oil tops $107 as gasoline hits $4.48 nationally; diesel export ban weighed

Brent settled above $105, diesel nears records, and the White House is considering banning diesel exports — a move oil executives warn would raise pump prices.
Unspun · Listen: 4:35

Oil prices surged Monday, gasoline hit new milestones, and the White House is weighing a step that could reshape fuel markets.

Brent crude futures jumped 2.6 percent to $107.02 a barrel intraday before settling nearly 1 percent higher at $105.28. West Texas Intermediate rose 2.7 percent to $94.91 intraday, settling slightly higher at $92.60, according to the New York Post. Barron's reported a larger intraday move — WTI up 4.1 percent to $96.27, taking September's increase to around 11.5 percent. The numbers differ because intraday and settlement snapshots come at different times; both agree the single-day jump was large.

At the pump, the national average for regular gasoline reached $4.48 a gallon Monday, per AAA — a near-10 percent increase from a month ago. Before the conflict the average was $2.98 in February and $3.13 last September. Diesel sits near $6.45 a gallon nationally, per Barron's, close to all-time highs and up more than 75 percent from a year ago; Los Angeles County diesel is approaching its all-time record.

Over the weekend, President Trump said he is “looking very seriously” at banning diesel exports, in a Fox News interview reported by Barron's. Major oil executives and Energy Secretary Chris Wright pushed back, arguing such a ban would raise domestic gas prices.

Diesel is the fuel that runs farms, freight trucks, and supply chains. Barron's notes economists warn energy costs could push the consumer price index above 4 percent and bleed into food and apparel prices. Markets felt it Monday morning: the Dow fell 349 points (0.7 percent), the S&P 500 dropped 0.8 percent, the Nasdaq 0.9 percent; the 10-year Treasury hit 5.263 percent and the 30-year 5.579 percent, per the New York Post.

What drove the move: the Post reported Trump rejected an Iranian peace proposal and expects to resume U.S. strikes after the November midterms. That framing rests on the Post's reporting of Trump's remarks; no other outlet independently confirmed it. But the underlying driver — the U.S.-Iran conflict pushing up oil and gas — is independently corroborated: ECB chief Christine Lagarde, speaking Monday in Brussels, attributed eurozone inflation pressure to “surging oil and gas prices, stemming from the US-Iran conflict,” per Reuters.

One analyst, Nic Puckrin of The Coin Bureau, notes the Strategic Petroleum Reserve is at its lowest since 1982 and refined-product supplies are being drawn down — one analyst's view, not official data, but a measure of how little cushion the system has. The White House has not issued a formal export-ban plan; the next data points are the weekly inventory reports and any White House statement on exports.

Sources: New York Post, Barron's, AAA, Fox News, Reuters, European Central Bank, CME Group
