Previously undisclosed Iranian missile attack injured eight U.S. Marines

NBC News reports a Sept. 14 cruise-missile strike in the Strait of Hormuz hurt eight Marines. The Pentagon had not publicly reported it; injuries were logged nearly 10 days later.
Unspun · Listen: 4:01

A U.S. military casualty event from two weeks ago is only now being reported. Eight U.S. Marines were injured in an Iranian cruise-missile attack in the Strait of Hormuz on September 14, and the public was not told at the time.

An Iranian anti-ship cruise missile struck a vessel in the Strait where eight Marines were serving, according to three U.S. officials cited by NBC News in a first report published Monday at 9:22 AM Eastern. Military.com carried an independent write-up the same day. The eight injured were seven enlisted Marines and one officer, serving as part of a battalion landing team.

Their injuries were smoke inhalation and possible traumatic brain injuries — concussion-like symptoms including headaches. Officials said the injuries were not classified as serious, and all eight returned to duty shortly after. Notably, the vessel was not a U.S. Navy ship; officials described it only as a “naval asset” or “maritime vessel” and declined to specify the type or flag.

The injuries were entered into the Pentagon's Defense Casualty Analysis System nearly 10 days after the attack. A U.S. official attributed the delay to symptoms that can appear days later and a tracking system lacking real-time updates.

There are limits to what is verified. The attack was not publicly confirmed by the Pentagon at the time of reporting — it rests on three anonymous U.S. officials speaking to NBC News. Military.com said it had reached out to the Defense Department for comment and was awaiting a response. The exact vessel type and flag are unknown by the officials' own refusal to specify.

The broader context: the war began February 28, and Monday marked the start of its seventh month, with no ceasefire reported. Pentagon data cited by Military.com put the conflict's toll at 18 dead and more than 800 wounded; the casualty system recorded 861 U.S. service members injured as of September 23. The Army also said Capt. Bianca C. Wilkerson, 37, of Norfolk, Va., died September 18 from a “non-combat related incident” and was posthumously promoted to major.

Delayed casualty reporting has been an acknowledged pattern throughout this war, so official totals remain subject to revision as later-symptom injuries get logged — as these eight were. The report, sourced to U.S. officials, also indicates Iran retained the ability to strike a U.S. military asset in the world's most critical oil chokepoint in mid-September, even amid a declared blockade.

Sources: NBC News, Military.com, Pentagon Defense Casualty Analysis System, U.S. Army, Jerusalem Post, Al Bawaba
