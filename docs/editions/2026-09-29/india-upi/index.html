India's Supreme Court declines to block new digital-payment fees

Starting Oct. 15, larger UPI merchant payments carry a 0.4% fee, capped at 300 rupees. Small transactions and person-to-person transfers stay free.
Unspun · Listen: 5:40

India's Supreme Court declined Monday to block new charges on digital payments, a decision affecting how hundreds of millions of people pay every day.

A three-judge bench headed by Chief Justice Surya Kant, with Justices Joymalya Bagchi and V. Mohana, declined to stay the new merchant discount rate framework and issued notices to the Union government, the Reserve Bank of India, and the National Payments Corporation of India, giving them four weeks to respond to a public interest litigation filed by advocate Anjan Datta. The case challenges the Centre's September 14 notification and the framework announced September 15. Reporting comes from TradingView and Moneycontrol.

The framework takes effect October 15, 2026: a 0.4 percent merchant discount rate on specified person-to-merchant UPI transactions above 2,000 rupees (about $21), capped at 300 rupees for transactions of 75,000 rupees and above. It ends nearly six years of fully free UPI — India's unified payments interface, the world's highest-volume instant-payment system and the primary way more than a billion people transact daily.

Exemptions: person-to-person transfers remain free regardless of amount; merchant payments up to 2,000 rupees remain free; merchants receiving up to 1 lakh rupees per month are exempt; essential sectors (railways, telecom, insurance, fuel, agricultural inputs) pay a flat 5 rupees per transaction; capital-market payments (mutual funds, securities, brokers) face 0.02 percent, capped at 300 rupees. The government says roughly 96 percent of person-to-merchant transactions remain unaffected — a self-reported figure, not independently audited.

The Solicitor General told the court that no fee money goes to the government — “merely a service charge levied by aggregators and banks.” The petitioner challenged the constitutional validity of amended Section 10A of the Payment and Settlement Systems Act, 2007, and questioned why UPI above 2,000 rupees is charged while RuPay debit cards keep no-charge protection. The petitioner's claim that costs could eventually be passed to consumers is argument, not fact; officials say merchants cannot pass it on, but the plea notes no disclosed cost studies exist.

The decision is procedural, not final: the court declined to freeze the framework before it takes effect, and the case continues with a four-week response deadline — after the fees have already started. Starting October 15, larger merchant payments carry a small capped fee while everyday transactions stay free. Merchants are weighing whether to refuse UPI for larger sales or split bills to stay under the 2,000-rupee threshold.

Sources: Supreme Court of India, TradingView, Moneycontrol, Solicitor General of India, Reserve Bank of India, National Payments Corporation of India
