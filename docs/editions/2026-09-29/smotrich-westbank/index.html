Israeli minister urges Gaza-style West Bank campaign; U.N. Council meets

Smotrich called for dismantling the Palestinian Authority weeks before Israel's election. The Security Council heard 6.7 settler attacks a day and 4,300 displaced.
Unspun · Listen: 4:04

Finance Minister Bezalel Smotrich said in a Ynet podcast interview published Sunday, September 27, that Israel should “go to war” in the West Bank and “do there what we did in Gaza.” He called for dismantling the Palestinian Authority — “which is a terror authority” — killing those he described as terrorists, confiscating weapons, and further restricting Palestinian movement on roads he called “our roads.” He also pointed to the depopulation of three Palestinian refugee camps in prior military operations.

The Times of Israel and Al Jazeera reported the remarks September 27-28. Context from the Times of Israel: Smotrich heads the far-right Religious Zionism party, serves as a Defense Ministry minister overseeing settlements, is campaigning ahead of Israel's October 27 general election, and has previously called for annexing more than 80 percent of the West Bank. The Palestinian Authority condemned his remarks as a call for escalation, annexation, and displacement. Whether the remarks are campaign rhetoric or a real signal is genuinely debated; Al Jazeera framed it as “rhetoric or real threat.” His words are a minister's stated position, not government policy.

On Monday, the UN Security Council met on the Middle East, and the numbers presented were stark. The UN Office for the Coordination of Humanitarian Affairs recorded more than 1,600 settler attacks in the West Bank this year — 6.7 a day, up from five a day in 2025 — with more than 4,300 Palestinians displaced. Israel invited bids for 1,234 homes in the E1 area near East Jerusalem, deadline October 19; UN officials warned the project would divide the West Bank.

In Gaza, the Council heard that 1,394 Palestinians have been killed since the October 2025 ceasefire, per UNRWA citing local health authorities, and that 94 percent of the population needs shelter assistance. Board of Peace official Nickolay Mladenov said conditions on the ground “do not yet match the documents.”

Separately Monday, Israeli strikes in Gaza killed at least two people — a woman on a Gaza City rooftop and a woman in the Nuseirat refugee camp — according to medics cited by Reuters. The Israeli military said it struck a militant in Nuseirat and did not comment on the Gaza City strike.

Running totals: more than 73,000 killed in Gaza since October 2023 per Palestinian authorities; at least 1,400 Palestinians killed since the ceasefire per Gaza health officials; four Israeli soldiers killed per the Israeli military. Gaza casualty figures come from Palestinian health authorities and cannot be independently verified by outside outlets.

Sources: Ynet, Times of Israel, Al Jazeera, Palestinian Authority, UN News, UN Office for the Coordination of Humanitarian Affairs, UNRWA, Reuters, Israeli military
