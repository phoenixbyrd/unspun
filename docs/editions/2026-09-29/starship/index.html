SpaceX Starship reaches orbit for the first time; flight cut short

Fourteenth test flight deployed 26 next-gen Starlink satellites before an engine shutdown ended the mission early over the Pacific.
Unspun · Listen: 6:05

SpaceX's Starship reached orbit for the first time on Monday, a milestone in the largest rocket ever built — though the flight was cut short by an engine problem.

The 14th full-scale Starship test flight launched Monday from Starbase, Texas, flying the Super Heavy v3 booster. The upper stage reached orbit for the first time; Mission Control announced: “Starship is orbital.” One engine shut down prematurely before orbit, and controllers flew on with the rest. SpaceX has not detailed the cause. Earlier flights proved the booster could return and the ship could survive reentry; in July a Starship was recovered from the Indian Ocean and inspected by hand, and this flight's heat shield was modified based on those findings.

The flight deployed 26 Starlink V3 satellites — the next generation, larger and more capable — joining about 11,000 older Starlinks already providing internet service. The planned 10-hour flight (six laps at 170 miles / 275 km, 17,500 mph) was cut short after a couple of laps; Starship splashed down north of Hawaii about three hours after liftoff instead of the planned Pacific site near Chile, tipping over and erupting in flames. SpaceX said the early end was “out of an abundance of caution” over the engine trouble.

NASA Administrator Jared Isaacman congratulated SpaceX on reaching orbit and “managing every step in a safe, responsible and especially inspirational way” — the agency's public read on a contractor it depends on for the moon program.

The 407-foot (124-meter) rocket is designed to be fully reusable. Next, per SpaceX and Elon Musk: the next Starship may return to the launch pad for a catch by the mechanical arms — Musk gives it even or slightly better odds, his own projection. A reflight is possible by year's end or early next year. A second launch site is nearing completion at Kennedy Space Center; a third is planned for Louisiana. Starship is meant to haul satellites in bulk, phasing out the Falcon 9 within several years.

The NASA thread: Artemis III could fly as soon as next summer — a triple-launch orbital docking exercise between an Orion capsule with astronauts, Jeff Bezos's Blue Moon lander flying first, and Musk's Starship. Artemis IV, the moon landing, is slated for no sooner than 2028, using whichever lander is ready first. Starship reaching orbit is a prerequisite for its role. Reporting: The Hindu BusinessLine (September 29, covering the September 28 flight; photos by Reuters' Steve Nesius), The Hindu's same-day AP account, SpaceX's update, and Isaacman's statement.
