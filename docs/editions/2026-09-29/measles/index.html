Pennsylvania measles outbreak passes 900 cases; death count disputed

903 cases and 176 hospitalized as of Monday. The state reported four deaths; the CDC website lists one verified measles death. The toll is unresolved.
Unspun · Listen: 5:38

Pennsylvania's measles outbreak has crossed 900 cases, the state's worst resurgence in decades, and officials disagree over how many deaths the virus caused.

Pennsylvania Department of Health data, reported by Reuters on Monday at 18:33 UTC, shows 68 new measles infections since Wednesday, September 23, bringing the total to 903 as of Monday, September 28. Hospitalizations reached 176, up from 164 on September 23 — the hospitalization count rising faster than the case count is the number epidemiologists watch. Lancaster County remains the hardest-hit area; outbreaks concentrate where vaccination coverage is lowest.

Pennsylvania reported four measles-related deaths among unvaccinated residents earlier this month. Governor Josh Shapiro and U.S. Health Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr. disagree over how many were directly caused by the virus. The CDC said it was reviewing how it classifies measles-related deaths; the CDC's website now lists one “verified measles death” without confirming where it occurred.

The distinction matters. “Directly caused by the virus” versus “related to the illness” are different classifications, and the classification changes the official count — a death in a measles patient from a secondary complication can be counted either way. The CDC's review means the standard itself is under reconsideration, and the death toll is genuinely unresolved: four reported by the state, one verified on the CDC website, two officials in disagreement.

Nationally, the CDC reports the United States has recorded 3,659 measles cases in 2026 as of September 24. Pennsylvania's 903 is a large share of that national total. Measles was declared eliminated in the United States in 2000; outbreaks since have been measured in dozens or low hundreds. Nine hundred three cases in one state is a different scale.

The medical facts: measles is a highly contagious viral disease causing fever, rash, and respiratory complications, preventable with the MMR vaccine — 97 percent protection after two doses, among the highest efficacy figures in vaccinology. Every case in this outbreak is, in the medical sense, preventable. The open questions are the death classification and whether the curve bends.

Sources: Reuters, Pennsylvania Department of Health, Centers for Disease Control and Prevention, Gov. Josh Shapiro, HHS Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr.
