Nor'easter finally dissipates; Northeast turns dry and warmer

The week-long storm is gone after roughly 200,000 outages and one death. Tuesday brings marked improvement, with highs into the 70s by week's end. Polo's remnants threaten the central U.S.
Unspun · Listen: 4:49

The nor'easter that spent nearly a week parked off the East Coast dissipated Tuesday, September 29, ending a storm that formed September 23 and battered the Mid-Atlantic and New England with days of heavy rain, damaging winds, and coastal flooding.

The storm's lowest pressure reached 986 hectopascals, and the highest gust recorded was 81 miles per hour at Montauk, New York. Roughly 200,000 power outages were recorded over the life of the storm. One person died and four were injured.

The death was 56-year-old Leighton Brown, a New York City Housing Authority employee killed in Brooklyn on Saturday when a tree fell on him, according to New York City officials. He had been working through the storm.

Monday was still a travel disaster in the Northeast. Across the United States there were 4,338 flight delays and 481 cancellations. Boston Logan alone posted 301 cancellations and an FAA ground stop in heavy wind. LaGuardia recorded 641 delays with a ground stop until 10:00 AM Eastern. Amtrak's Boston-New York service was suspended, and tens of thousands of customers remained without power Monday morning as crews worked through fallen trees and downed lines.

Wind reports illustrated the storm's reach: Surf City, New Jersey at 74 mph; Harvey Cedars, New Jersey at 69; Nantucket at 59; Wellfleet on Cape Cod at 58; Kennedy Airport at 56. A full moon over the weekend worsened coastal flooding along already inundated shorelines.

The forecast at publish time is markedly better. The National Weather Service expects the decaying storm to exit Tuesday with winds shifting northwest; rain tapered off late Monday night, clouds may linger through Tuesday afternoon, and Wednesday could bring the first real sunshine. Boston sees a high of 66 today; by week's end highs should reach the 70s to near 80, above normal. Rhode Island, which took 2 to 6 inches of rain, expects mainly dry and warmer weather through Friday.

Elsewhere in the tropics, Tropical Storms Fay and Hanna are spinning in the central Atlantic, neither threatening land. The storm to watch is Hurricane Polo in the East Pacific: 6 to 12 inches of rain over parts of Mexico, with its remnants expected to combine with a non-tropical system and bring heavy rain and possible flash flooding to the central United States later this week. The Atlantic has still not produced a single hurricane this season, the latest such start in the satellite era, according to AccuWeather's Alex DaSilva.
