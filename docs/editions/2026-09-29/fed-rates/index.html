Markets price 70% odds of October Fed rate hike; mortgages top 7%

Surging energy prices have traders betting on another hike. The 30-year mortgage is at its highest since January 2025, and Friday's jobs report is the next test.
Unspun · Listen: 4:03

Markets are now betting heavily that the Federal Reserve will raise interest rates again next month. The CME Group's FedWatch tool pegs the odds of an October hike at about 70 percent, with December hike odds at roughly 92 percent, according to Barron's reporting Monday. Those are market-priced probabilities, not a Fed decision.

The backdrop: on September 16, the Federal Open Market Committee voted 12-0 to raise the federal funds target to 3.75-4.00 percent, the first hike since July 2023. The dot plot median implies rates reach 4.1 percent by end-2026.

What moved the odds: energy prices. With oil settling above $105 a barrel Monday, economists warn inflation could accelerate. Citi Research economists Andrew Hollenhorst and Veronica Clark, in a report published September 28, project September nonfarm payrolls of about 85,000 with unemployment edging from 4.1 to 4.2 percent. They call the September core CPI print the decisive variable for October; only negative payrolls or unemployment jumping to 4.3 percent would force markets to reassess.

Borrowing costs are already at striking levels. The 30-year fixed mortgage rate is averaging more than 7.0 percent, the highest since January 2025, according to the Freddie Mac Primary Mortgage Market Survey.

EY-Parthenon chief economist Gregory Daco, in his September 28 newsletter, wrote that interest-rate changes “cannot produce energy, resolve supply chain pressures, expand the labor force or increase semiconductor capacity.” He expects an additional quarter-point hike in December, projects full-year 2026 inflation at 2.9 percent, and notes food prices up 2.7 percent year over year as of August. Fed Governor Lisa Cook said Monday the labor market “appears to be well positioned to handle an increase in rates.”

The watch items: Friday's September jobs report, then the inflation prints. If inflation runs hot, the Fed has room in its own projection to reach 4.1 percent by year's end. If the data cools, markets will reprice just as fast. The 70 percent figure is the collective wager of traders, not inside information — and in past cycles these odds have swung sharply on a single release.

Sources: CME Group FedWatch, Barron's, Freddie Mac, Citi Research, EY-Parthenon, Federal Reserve, Federal Open Market Committee
