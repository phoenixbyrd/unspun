EU sanctions over deported Ukrainian children; sabotage warnings issued

Ten people and 17 entities sanctioned, including a North Korean camp. Kaja Kallas and NATO's top commander in Europe warned Russia is planning more sabotage.
Unspun · Listen: 5:41

EU member countries on Monday imposed sanctions on 10 individuals and 17 entities for involvement in the unlawful deportation of Ukrainian children to Russia, Reuters reported. The list includes Moscow's City Tourism Committee, children's and sports camps in Russia, and the Songdowon International Children's Camp in North Korea, where the European Council said Ukrainian children are brought to promote pro-Russian narratives.

Before a meeting of EU defense ministers in Brussels, foreign policy chief Kaja Kallas warned that Russia is planning more sabotage: “We need to be vigilant because we see in the intelligence that Russia is planning more sabotage acts and attacks against our democracy — to divide our society, to intimidate our societies into refraining to help Ukraine.” Her assessment rests on intelligence she cited; it is not independently verified evidence.

Separately Monday, NATO Supreme Allied Commander Europe Alexus Grynkewich told EU lawmakers that Russia's goal with hybrid attacks is “to break the will of Ukraine's allies” to keep supporting Ukraine, and urged member states to publicly attribute attacks when confident. He cited an explosives-laden drone found at Leipzig Airport in early August and a Russian frigate firing signal flares at a Danish helicopter in mid-September. Independent verification beyond his statement was thin.

On the Polish-Ukrainian border, the Dorohusk-Yahodyn crossing suspended traffic three times in one day, with one evacuation, amid a pattern of alerts. That pattern follows two Russian drones exploding on the Ukrainian side on September 13. Dorohusk mayor Wojciech Sawa was quoted in the reporting.

A secondary outlet reported that the EU Council also approved five joint defense projects Monday — drones and counter-drones, maritime and underwater infrastructure, space, air and missile defense, and the eastern flank. That rests on a single secondary source and should be treated as thin.

The day's through-line: sanctions targeting a specific rights issue, the deportation of children, alongside a coordinated message from the EU's top diplomat and NATO's top commander in Europe that more sabotage is coming and that allies should name perpetrators publicly when attribution is confident. The war in Ukraine is now four and a half years old, and Monday's moves show Europe's security posture shifting from reacting to incidents to warning of the next ones.

Sources: Reuters, European Council, Kaja Kallas, NATO, Alexus Grynkewich, Wojciech Sawa
