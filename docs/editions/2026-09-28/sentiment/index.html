Consumer sentiment falls to near-record low in September

The Michigan index hits 48.1 — second-lowest ever — as inflation, gas prices, and borrowing costs squeeze households.
Unspun · Listen: 3:35

The University of Michigan's final September reading of consumer sentiment registered 48.1, the second-lowest final reading in the survey's 74-year history, according to data released September 25 and reported by ABC Audio. The index hit its all-time low of 44.8 in May, shortly after the Iran war began, recovered to 51.7 by August, and fell again in September.

The May reading of 44.8 coincided with the start of the Iran war, and sentiment had been recovering through the summer before September's reversal. The September decline hit both parties' supporters, an unusual breadth that Hsu highlighted in the survey release.

Surveys of Consumers director Joanne Hsu said Democrats and Republicans alike showed sizable declines in September. The drop was driven by prices: consumer prices rose 3.4 percent annually in August, according to Bureau of Labor Statistics data, and the national average gas price stood at $4.49 per gallon per AAA, roughly 50 percent higher than before the war began in late February.

Borrowing costs added to the squeeze. A key long-term Treasury rate hit its highest level in nearly two decades on September 23, days after the Federal Reserve raised its benchmark borrowing rate. Investors put the odds of another quarter-point Fed rate increase next month at about two in three, according to the CME FedWatch Tool. Federal Reserve Chair Kevin Warsh said at a press conference last week, 'The plain fact is that inflation is too high and has been for too long.'

Sentiment is a survey measure, not an economic outcome, and it coexists with some stronger hard data: employers added 162,000 workers in August, according to the Bureau of Labor Statistics. But the survey reflects how paychecks, gas prices, and borrowing costs feel to households right now.

Sources: University of Michigan Surveys of Consumers, ABC Audio, Bureau of Labor Statistics, AAA, CME FedWatch
