Two mass shootings in South African townships kill at least 27

Seventeen dead at a Johannesburg-area bar, ten more near Cape Town — at least 38 killed in less than a week.
Unspun · Listen: 4:04

Two separate mass shootings in South African townships within hours left at least 27 people dead from Saturday night into Sunday morning, according to the Associated Press and the South African Police Service.

In Wedela township, southwest of Johannesburg, at about 9:30 p.m. Saturday, eight gunmen armed with AK-47 rifles and pistols opened fire at a bar, first at patrons outside, then storming inside. Seventeen people were killed, 13 men and 4 women, and at least 15 were wounded. The attackers searched some victims, stole mobile phones, set two vehicles on fire, and fled in two cars.

In Lwandle township near Cape Town, at about 1:45 a.m. Sunday, gunmen attacked a barbecue restaurant and store. Ten people were killed and 11 wounded. Police are searching for multiple suspects; the exact number has not been disclosed.

No arrests had been made in either attack as of Sunday, and specialized police units were deployed. Deputy provincial police commissioner Maj. Gen. Fred Kekana said the suspects 'appear to have carried out the attack in retaliation for an incident last month,' in which one person was killed and another wounded; one Saturday victim was being sought by police in connection with that earlier incident. That retaliation link is a police assessment, not an established fact, and police have confirmed no connection between the two weekend attacks.

Five days earlier, on September 22, a mass shooting at a house party in KwaMakhutha township near Durban killed at least 11 people, including a pregnant woman. The total stands at at least 38 dead in less than a week. South Africa records roughly 60 homicides per day in a population of 62 million, according to national statistics cited by the New York Post. Experts say multi-gunman mass shootings are becoming more common in poorer communities, linked to criminal gangs and illegal firearms.

Sources: Associated Press, South African Police Service
