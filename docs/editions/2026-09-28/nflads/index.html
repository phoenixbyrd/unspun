Third taxpayer-funded White House ad airs during Sunday NFL games

Stamped 'Paid for by the US Government,' the ad aired during two Fox NFL games; funding details have not been disclosed.
Unspun · Listen: 3:48

A third White House-funded television ad aired Sunday during at least two NFL games broadcast by Fox: the Washington Commanders-Seattle Seahawks matchup and the Carolina Panthers-Cleveland Browns game, according to CNN and the media tracker AdImpact.

The ad shows imagery of President Trump walking down a hall over audio of him saying 'this is the final battle,' and includes: 'We will cast out the communist, Marxists, and fascists. We will throw off the sick political class that hates our country. We will rout the fake news media, and we will liberate America from these villains once and for all,' echoing remarks from his Veterans Day speech in 2023. The ad is stamped 'Paid for by the US Government' at the end. CNN reports it appears nearly identical to most of a video Trump posted on Truth Social in January 2024 during his third presidential campaign; the Truth Social version has six additional seconds including his campaign logo and a text-to number.

The first ad began airing September 23, with rapidly edited images of Trump denouncing communism and socialism alongside text touting administration accomplishments such as 'reigniting American manufacturing.' The second features Trump at Mount Rushmore with his voice saying, 'Never before has any nation celebrated so magnificent a triumph as this one.'

The White House has not said which budget line funded the ads, but compared them to past public-service announcements on Medicare, Obamacare, and Covid-19 vaccination, saying in a statement the new commercials 'are very clearly not campaign ads.' A spokesperson called the first ad 'educational and unapologetically patriotic' and part of efforts to 'promote national pride.'

Common Cause suggested the first ad could violate rules against using taxpayer dollars for propaganda. Vice president for policy Carol Evans said, 'I don't think you have to be a legal scholar and wonder why the president has to use taxpayer funds to promote his image' this close to an election. The legality question is a watchdog suggestion, not an established finding, and funding details have not been disclosed. The midterm elections are in November, a little over a month away.

Sources: CNN, AdImpact, Common Cause
