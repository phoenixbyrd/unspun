Bitget loses $387.5 million in hack; THORChain refuses to block attacker wallets

Nearly $400 million in customer funds is being laundered in the open while a core crypto protocol refuses to intervene.
Unspun · Listen: 4:23

Crypto exchange Bitget says assets worth approximately $387.5 million were transferred to attacker-controlled addresses on September 24, up from an initial estimate of $351.6 million. The revision added Zcash and TRON transfers from the original window, not a new theft.

Unauthorized transfers from hot and warm wallet infrastructure were detected at 18:31 UTC; CEO Gracy Chen posted a security notice at 21:30 UTC and withdrawals were paused. In a September 25 technical update, Chen said the attacker compromised a backend wallet system and used spoofed transaction data to bypass the exchange's own authorization checks; a private key compromise was ruled out. Chen said investigators found patterns consistent with earlier operations by North Korean hacking groups, though attribution is not final and Bitget has not published a final attribution.

On September 26, Chen posted on X a formal request to THORChain, a decentralized cross-chain swap protocol: 'Our attacker addresses are publicly listed and actively tracked. We are formally asking @THORChain to refuse service to these addresses. Decentralization is a design principle, not a shield for facilitating known stolen funds. The industry is watching.' THORChain replied: 'THORChain is decentralized and permissionless like Bitcoin, Ethereum, and BNB Chain. What responsibility should Bitcoin, Ethereum, and BNB Chain bear when handling known stolen funds?' It declined to blacklist the addresses, and no node vote to pause the flows had been recorded as of September 28.

On-chain data reported by TRM Labs, Bitquery, GoPlus Security, and THORChain's Midgard indexer show the stolen funds were converted from ETH, BNB, TRX, and XRP into native Bitcoin through THORChain. Bitquery counted 126.71 BTC paid out across 66 THORChain swaps by September 25; about 102.98 million XRP was the largest single asset taken; and at 3:14 a.m. Eastern on Sunday, September 27, another 9,999 XRP left a published attacker address and was swapped into Bitcoin. GoPlus estimated roughly $8.5 million (101.5 BTC) had exited via THORChain and about $43 million (27.63 million XRP) was mid-swap. The attacker also used Uniswap, 1inch, Stargate, Across, Relay, Chainflip, and Circle's CCTP.

TRM Labs says Bitget's approximately $464 million User Protection Fund covers the loss, and Bitget offers a recovery bounty of 5 percent of frozen or recovered funds. Some assets have been frozen through coordination with exchanges, so far a small share. Bitget began a phased withdrawal restart with Bitcoin at 8:00 UTC on September 28, running through October 2. OKX founder Star Xu countered THORChain's decentralization defense, arguing the network halted itself for 39 days after a May 2026 exploit drained $10.7 million from its own vaults, and noting THORChain retired its admin key in February 2025.

Sources: CryptoTimes, Bitget, TRM Labs, Bitquery, GoPlus Security
