Nor'easter lingers over the East Coast into Monday

More than 130,000 without power, flights canceled, and a full moon worsening coastal flooding as the storm lingers into Monday.
Unspun · Listen: 3:29

The nor'easter drifting near the New Jersey coastline continued battering the Mid-Atlantic and New England through Sunday and is expected to linger into Monday, according to the National Weather Service. Gusts topping 70 miles per hour were recorded in Surf City, New Jersey, and coastal cities from New Jersey to Massachusetts saw four to six inches of rain.

More than 130,000 customers were without power as of Sunday afternoon, according to weather.com's timestamped figures, though outage totals fluctuated across sources through the day. A 56-year-old New York City Housing Authority employee, Leighton Brown, was killed in Brooklyn on Saturday when a tree fell on him, according to New York City officials.

Travel remained disrupted. FlightAware data showed 130 flights canceled at Boston Logan airport alone, with delays exceeding four hours at some airports and airlines issuing travel waivers. A full moon this weekend compounded the damage, worsening coastal flooding along the Jersey Shore and New England coast.

The governors of New York and New Jersey declared states of emergency on Friday, and Connecticut activated its Emergency Operations Center, postures that remained in effect through Sunday. The National Weather Service warned that the combination of the full moon and storm surge would keep coastal flooding dangerous even as rainfall eases, with some coastal flooding possible during high tides into Monday.

The National Weather Service expects the storm to stay parked over the region through Monday before pulling away. Rain showers and gusty winds are forecast to linger over New England, Long Island, the Hudson Valley, and New York City on Monday, with conditions clearing late Monday into Tuesday. Tuesday is expected to be warmer and drier, with highs in the 70s.

Sources: National Weather Service, weather.com, USA Today, AccuWeather, FlightAware, New York City officials
