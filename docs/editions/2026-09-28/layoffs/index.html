Tech layoffs pass 225,000 in 2026 as companies cite AI

AI is now the most-cited reason for cuts, even at profitable companies like Oracle — and the trend is spreading beyond tech.
Unspun · Listen: 3:54

Tracked U.S. technology layoffs for 2026 reached 225,122 workers across 519 events as of September 28, according to SkillSyncer's layoffs tracker, as reported by Startup Fortune. Layoffs.fyi, which tracks a narrower group of venture-backed and public tech companies, counted 128,536 cuts across 299 companies as of September 10, already exceeding all of 2025's 122,606 cuts.

Artificial intelligence is now the stated reason. Challenger, Gray & Christmas reports AI was cited in 87,714 job cuts through May 2026 alone, more than the 54,836 AI-attributed cuts for all of 2025. AI led all stated reasons for layoffs for the third straight month in May, with 38,579 cuts that month, the highest monthly AI-attributed total since the firm began tracking the category in 2023.

Oracle accounts for the largest single entry: a 30,000-worker cut listed March 31. In a September 11 filing, Oracle described a fiscal 2026 restructuring plan aimed at improving efficiency 'including through the adoption and integration of artificial intelligence,' with an estimated cost of up to $2.1 billion as of August 31 plus roughly $700 million added after that date. September WARN filings listed 441 Bay Area positions. Oracle is not losing money: first-quarter fiscal 2027 net income was $4.7 billion, and remaining performance obligations reached $664 billion after more than $30 billion in new AI cloud contracts.

The cuts are concentrated in the Bay Area, where about 13,900 tech positions have been eliminated so far in 2026, up 36.6 percent from all of 2025, according to a hiddenjobs.dev tally, with Meta leading Bay Area cuts at 3,715 disclosed positions. Meanwhile venture funding tells the other side of the story: PitchBook-NVCA data show U.S. venture deal value of $412.7 billion in the first half of 2026, with AI companies taking $355.9 billion of it.

Other September cuts include 286 jobs in Microsoft's Xbox studio consolidation, part of a 3,200-headcount-reduction goal by the end of fiscal 2027; 58 Seattle headquarters jobs at Expedia; and fewer than 250 IT workers at PeaceHealth, which is outsourcing to Tech Mahindra and HCI Group. The trackers are independent aggregators of announced cuts, not government data, and Challenger's AI figures run through May. The pattern of profitable companies cutting workers while citing AI now reaches beyond tech into engineering, customer support, content moderation, and data analysis.

Sources: SkillSyncer, Startup Fortune, Layoffs.fyi, Challenger, Gray & Christmas, Oracle (SEC filing)
