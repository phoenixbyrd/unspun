White House urges China to execute fentanyl precursor makers

Per a White House summit summary, the U.S. pressed Beijing for 'maximum sentences, including the death penalty' for precursor makers.
Unspun · Listen: 4:08

The Trump administration said it 'strongly encouraged' China to execute Chinese criminals who manufacture and distribute the precursor chemicals used to make illegal fentanyl, according to a White House release summarizing last week's three-day Washington summit with Chinese leader Xi Jinping, as reported by the New York Post on September 27.

The release stated: 'In August 2026, acting on information from U.S. law enforcement, China arrested 21 Chinese citizens for the manufacture and distribution of precursor chemicals to illicit drug producers in North America.' It continued: 'The United States strongly encouraged maximum sentences, including the death penalty where applicable.'

China's additional overtures, per the White House release: new export controls on two precursor chemicals shipped to North American producers, and classification of orphines, synthetic opioids described as up to 10 times more potent than fentanyl, as deadly drugs. China had already rolled out tougher guidelines on fentanyl-related substances ahead of the summit. China is identified as the top supplier of the precursor chemicals that Mexican cartels use to produce fentanyl, methamphetamine, and nitazenes, partly because of its dominance in chemical manufacturing.

Fentanyl killed nearly 74,000 Americans in 2022, its peak year, and opioid-related overdose deaths fell to 44,564 in 2025, according to the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention. The summit also produced other outcomes per the White House: tariff relief on about $30 billion of goods, a gift of two more giant pandas, an AI notification system, and China's push to boost petroleum refinement.

The New York Post is the single verified source for the White House release's wording, and independent corroboration of its exact text is thin; the '10 times more potent' characterization comes from the release as quoted by the Post. Prior administrations have also pressured Beijing on precursors, making this a continuity rather than a first.

Sources: New York Post, White House, Centers for Disease Control and Prevention
