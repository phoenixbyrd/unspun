California gas prices near all-time records

LA County at $6.40 after a 40-day streak, 9 cents from the all-time record; the national average is the highest ever this late in the year.
Unspun · Listen: 3:38

Los Angeles County's average price of regular gasoline rose for the 40th consecutive day on Sunday, climbing 1.3 cents to $6.40, its highest since October 10, 2022 and just 9.3 cents below the all-time record of $6.493 set October 5, 2022, according to AAA and Oil Price Information Service data reported by MyNewsLA. Orange County rose for the 40th straight day to $6.385, its highest since October 7, 2022 and 7.3 cents below its record $6.458.

Los Angeles County prices are up 75.8 cents over the 40-day streak, 19.8 cents over the past week, 69.5 cents over the past month, $1.675 over a year ago, and $1.706 since the Iran war began February 28. Orange County is up 81 cents over 40 days and $1.749 since the war's start.

California's streak reflects the state's persistently higher fuel costs layered on top of the national run-up tied to the Strait of Hormuz chokepoint and refinery pressures established in earlier reporting. The 40-day climb has added roughly three-quarters of a dollar per gallon in both Los Angeles and Orange counties.

The national average declined 0.8 cents to $4.479, but GasBuddy head of petroleum analysis Patrick De Haan says it is at its highest level this late in the calendar year. Nationally, prices are up 38 cents over the past month, $1.342 over a year ago, and $1.497 since the Iran attack.

Brent crude closed Friday around $95.52 per barrel, compared with $72.48 the day before the war began. In Sunday trading, West Texas Intermediate stood at $92.41 and Brent at $97.44.

Sources: AAA, Oil Price Information Service, MyNewsLA, GasBuddy
