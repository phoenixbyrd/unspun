Phoenix builders offer record incentives as home sales sink

Average incentives top $60,000 — mostly rate buydowns — as August builder sales hit their lowest of the year.
Unspun · Listen: 3:49

Metro Phoenix homebuilders are offering an average of more than $60,000 in incentives per new home, the highest average since the Great Recession, according to Jim Belfiore, CEO of housing research firm Belfiore Analytics, in Arizona Republic reporting. The incentives are mostly mortgage-rate buydowns and closing-cost credits.

The most generous markets include Cave Creek and Carefree at $175,000 on average, Scottsdale and Fountain Hills at $143,000, and South Phoenix at about $41,000. Builder sales totaled 1,317 homes in August, the lowest monthly total this year, while Valley resales of 5,471 were the lowest since March, according to ARMLS data.

The average new-home price was $624,980 in August versus $628,518 in July; the average resale price was $589,355 versus $606,907. The average 30-year fixed mortgage rate stood at 7.03 percent, according to Freddie Mac. A two-percentage-point full-term rate buydown on a $500,000 mortgage costs about $40,000, according to Rocket Mortgage.

Cromford Report senior housing analyst Tina Tamboer said the market would pick up when rates fall below 6.5 percent. Belfiore forecast that 2026 is 'very likely to be remembered as the worst in as many as 15 years for the housing industry,' and advised agents to have sellers offer rate buydowns to compete with builders, noting that 'resellers are struggling more than homebuilders.' Oakwood Homes Arizona market president Michael Fraley said first-time and price-sensitive buyers consider rates too high and prices unaffordable, and his company is offering buydowns, down-payment assistance, and low-down-payment financing.

The Belfiore Analytics figures are private research data, not government statistics, and the 'worst in 15 years' characterization is Belfiore's forecast. Still, the pattern signals returning buyer leverage, a bellwether for overheated Sun Belt markets.

Sources: Arizona Republic/azcentral, Belfiore Analytics, ARMLS, Freddie Mac, Cromford Report
