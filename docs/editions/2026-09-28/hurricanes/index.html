Hurricane Nolo skirts Hawaii; Category 3 Polo heads for Baja California

Nolo brings flooding to the Big Island and Maui; Category 3 Polo reaches Baja California on Monday, then threatens the U.S. Southwest.
Unspun · Listen: 4:01

Two Pacific hurricanes menaced the region Sunday, one skirting Hawaii and one bearing down on Mexico's Baja California. On Saturday, Hurricane Nolo churned very slowly south of Hawaii's Big Island with 90-mile-per-hour sustained winds, its center about 165 miles south-southwest of Hawaii's southernmost point. By Sunday, winds had risen to near 105 miles per hour, making it a Category 2 storm, moving away west at about 12 miles per hour, about 260 miles southwest of Hawaii's southernmost point.

Nolo is forecast to strengthen to 130 miles per hour, a major hurricane, by September 28 while staying well south and southwest of the islands, moving toward the Papahanaumokuakea Marine National Monument. The hurricane watch was canceled and no direct landfall is expected. But rain bands crossed the Big Island and Maui, with storm totals through September 27 of 10 to 15 inches and isolated totals of 20 inches. Warnings for life-threatening flooding and mudslides remained in effect, along with high wind warnings with gusts to 75 miles per hour for parts of the Big Island and Maui County, and large damaging waves along the Big Island coastline.

The Pacific season has been extremely active, driven by El Nino conditions. Forecasters expect Nolo to keep strengthening as it moves away from Hawaii, while Polo's northward track puts Baja California's resort and fishing communities in the path of a major hurricane on Monday.

More than 120 National Guard members were activated on the Big Island and about 400 statewide after the governor's emergency proclamation. President Trump approved an emergency declaration authorizing FEMA coordination. Hawaii is still recovering from Hurricane Lala, whose winds swept about 100 homes off their foundations on the Big Island. This is an El Nino-driven, extremely active Pacific season.

Meanwhile, Hurricane Polo was a Category 3 storm with 125-mile-per-hour sustained winds Sunday evening, moving north at 12 miles per hour about 270 miles west of Cabo San Lucas. Polo is expected to reach Baja California on Monday, bringing storm surge, high winds, and heavy rain capable of producing life-threatening flooding and mudslides. The storm is forecast to cross Baja and hit the Mexican mainland, then bring heavy rain and flooding to parts of the U.S. Southwest later this week, according to the Associated Press and the National Hurricane Center. Nolo's exact peak and Polo's Southwest impacts are still forecasts, not observed outcomes.

Sources: National Hurricane Center, Associated Press, USA Today, Hawaii Emergency Management Agency
