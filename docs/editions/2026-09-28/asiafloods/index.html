Deadly flooding across India, Nepal, and Thailand; tolls rise

Fifty-six dead in India, fourteen in Nepal, Bangkok's 50 districts declared disaster areas after 12 inches of rain in 48 hours.
Unspun · Listen: 4:17

Deadly flooding swept across South and Southeast Asia over the weekend, with updated tolls rising in India, Nepal, and Thailand, according to Reuters reporting updated September 27.

In India, floods and landslides killed 56 people, up from 15 in Saturday's early reports, with 46 injured and more than 1,000 houses damaged. Uttar Pradesh recorded 66.5 millimeters of rain from September 25 to 27, compared with a normal 7.6 millimeters. Rescue operations continued in waterlogged areas, and crop-damage assessments were ordered. India's Central Water Commission said 30 river-monitoring stations across seven states indicated a 'severe situation,' with the Ganga, Gandak, Kosi, Bagmati, and Narmada rivers above danger levels.

In Nepal, 14 people died and 11 were missing. Four people, three children and a woman, died when a landslide buried their house in Nishi Khola village in Baglung district. An avalanche buried the base camp of Himlung Himal, a 7,126-meter peak in Manang district; the 10 missing are Nepali workers who had been setting up tents for foreign climbers, who were not present. Flash-flood warnings were in effect in 49 of 77 districts. Power was fully disrupted in Manang, Mustang, Myagdi, and Baglung, and flooding of the Rahuganga River damaged a 40-megawatt hydropower project and transmission towers. The same areas were hit by catastrophic floods last month that killed more than 1,450 people, with thousands still missing, according to disaster official Shanti Mahat.

In Thailand, all 50 districts of Bangkok were declared disaster-affected areas after nearly 300 millimeters, or 12 inches, of rain fell in 48 hours. As of September 27, about 52,000 households in the capital were affected, 31 roads remained flooded, and 233 temporary shelters were prepared with about 4,200 people using them. Floodwater reached waist height inside homes in some communities. Eight deaths were recorded nationwide over the past 10 days. Thai Airways ground operations at Suvarnabhumi Airport were disrupted September 26 and 27, with flight delays and baggage backlogs; arriving passengers waited five to six hours at baggage reclaim, and ticketing assistance was extended through October 11. Rain eased Sunday, though canals remained high.

Casualty figures are 'at least' counts still being updated, and the Nepal avalanche rescue remains a developing situation.

Sources: Reuters, Associated Press, Thailand Department of Disaster Prevention and Mitigation, Thai Airways
