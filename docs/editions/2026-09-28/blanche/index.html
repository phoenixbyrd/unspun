Attorney general defends barring CNN from Air Force One pool

On ABC's 'This Week,' the attorney general called White House access 'a privilege, not a right' — without evidence for his inaccuracy claim.
Unspun · Listen: 3:35

Attorney General Todd Blanche appeared on ABC News's 'This Week' on Sunday and defended the White House's decision to bar CNN from boarding Air Force One for President Trump's September 26 trip to Neyland Stadium for the University of Texas-University of Tennessee football game. CNN had been scheduled to travel as the television pool reporter; the five-network rotation of ABC, CBS, CNN, NBC, and Fox takes turns covering the president and sharing feeds. CNN White House correspondent Alayna Treene reported the network was not on the list of outlets traveling with the president.

'The White House routinely decides who can go on Air Force One... It's not unusual, and as a matter of fact, it's common every time the president travels that he and his team decide which reporters get to travel with the president,' Blanche told co-anchor Martha Raddatz, according to USA Today. Defending the broader credential fight, he said, 'Being in the White House is a privilege. It's not a right.' He added that 'at some point, complete misinformation and the inaccuracy of one-sided news of certain organizations does become a national security issue,' without elaborating on which reporting he meant or how it posed a national security concern.

The pool system exists so a small rotating group of reporters can witness presidential events on behalf of the entire press corps, sharing notes, photos, and video feeds with outlets that are not present. Exclusion from the pool therefore limits what the broader public can see of a presidential trip, independent of the separate question of White House credentials.

The context: on September 18, Trump announced bans on CNN, MS NOW, and Politico from the White House. On September 24, U.S. District Judge Tim Kelly issued a 14-day temporary restraining order reinstating press passes for reporters at all three outlets. The administration continues to litigate, and the Air Force One pool exclusion is a separate access question from the credential ban.

The Guardian reported that Blanche said the administration banned the three outlets beginning September 19 because the president was 'sick and tired' of their reporting, and that the judge's order Thursday restored their credentials. Blanche offered no evidence that the outlets' reporting was inaccurate rather than merely unflattering, and the Guardian noted he could not point to a specific report when pressed. The legal dispute over the credential bans is ongoing, and pool access determines what the public sees of the presidency.

Sources: USA Today, ABC News, The Guardian
