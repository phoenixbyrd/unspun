Five arrested near RAF Fairford on explosives and terrorism suspicion

Three suspicious vehicles intercepted two miles from the U.S. bomber base; 85 households evacuated; no charges yet.
Unspun · Listen: 4:02

British police arrested five men early Sunday near RAF Fairford, the Gloucestershire air base used by the United States Air Force, on suspicion of explosives and terrorism offenses. At about 12:45 a.m. British time, Gloucestershire Police received a public emergency call reporting three suspicious vehicles heading toward the base. Armed officers intercepted them in the village of Whelford, about two miles from the base.

The men were initially detained on suspicion of offenses under the Explosives Act of 1875 and then further arrested on suspicion of preparing a terrorist act. They remain in custody; British counter-terrorism law allows holding suspects without charge for up to 14 days. Counter Terrorism Policing has taken over the investigation.

A major incident was declared. A 400-meter cordon was placed around the vehicles, and about 85 households in Whelford were evacuated to a temporary shelter at a nearby leisure center. British Army explosive ordnance disposal teams used a robot to examine the vans; large black barrels and other objects were removed from one van, and CNN reporters near the perimeter reported what sounded like two controlled detonations.

As of September 28, no names, charges, or motive have been disclosed, and no one has been charged. Police have not confirmed that RAF Fairford was the intended target, and tests are ongoing on whether the recovered materials were working explosives.

RAF Fairford hosts U.S. Air Force units including heavy bombers and has been used since March 2026 for U.S. operations against Iran. Days earlier, base spotters reported a raised security posture. The U.S. military said it was 'working closely' with British counterparts. A second U.S.-linked base, RAF Lakenheath, was reportedly raised to the U.K.'s 'Charlie' alert level after the arrests, according to unconfirmed reports. Prime Minister Andy Burnham publicly acknowledged the incident on September 27 and thanked police and armed forces.

Sources: Gloucestershire Police, Counter Terrorism Policing, The Aviationist, TWZ
