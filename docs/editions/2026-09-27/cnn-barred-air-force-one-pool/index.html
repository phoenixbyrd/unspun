CNN barred from Air Force One pool trip amid ongoing press-access lawsuit

The exclusion follows a ban announcement, credential seizures, a lawsuit, and a court order to restore access.
Unspun · Listen: 4:20

CNN was not permitted to travel with the president on Air Force One on Saturday for his trip to the Tennessee-Texas college football game in Knoxville. CNN had been slated as that day's television pool representative. The networks rotate (ABC, CBS, CNN, NBC, Fox) and share camera feeds. The White House's released guidance for the trip omitted CNN. Saturday morning the president said, "I want real news. I love an open, free press. What I don't like is the fake press," naming CNN and MSNBC.

The timeline: September 18, the president announced on Truth Social that CNN, MSNBC, and Politico were banned from the White House. September 19, their journalists were denied entry and had press credentials taken by the Secret Service. September 21, the three outlets sued the president and three White House officials. A federal judge then ruled the administration must temporarily restore access. The outlets were nevertheless excluded from Thursday's Xi state dinner and Saturday's Air Force One trip.

The White House press pool is a rotation in which a small group of journalists travels with the president and shares reporting, photos, and video with all outlets, because hundreds of journalists cannot fit on Air Force One or in a small room. Removing a network from the rotation leaves the event covered but removes one independent editorial voice from the room.

There have been fights over access in every administration. The administration's position is that access to the president is a privilege, not a right. The outlets argue the First Amendment bars the government from punishing journalists for the content of their coverage, a well-established principle. The unresolved question is how far it extends to discretionary access like Air Force One versus White House grounds generally. The scope of the judge's order was not detailed in the coverage, and the constitutional question remains before the courts.

Sources: Reuters, court filings
