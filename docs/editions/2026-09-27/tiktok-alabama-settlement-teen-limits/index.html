TikTok settles with Alabama for at least $100 million, agrees to teen usage limits

Teen limits include a two-hour daily cap and an overnight block. At least 27 other states have similar suits.
Unspun · Listen: 3:39

Alabama announced Friday, September 25, that it had reached a settlement with TikTok and its parent company ByteDance, resolving claims the platform endangered children and misled consumers about safety. The agreement came days before a trial scheduled to begin Monday, September 28, in Montgomery state court. Attorney General Steve Marshall, who sued the company last year, called it "a great day for Alabama parents."

Under the agreement, TikTok will impose new usage limits for Alabama teenagers: a two-hour daily time limit, pauses after 15 minutes of use, stricter age checks, restricted overnight access, and suspended notifications during school hours. TikTok will also ban beauty filters for teenagers and offer young users a nonpersonalized content feed. The overnight block runs midnight to 6 a.m., with a conditional expansion to 10 p.m. to 7 a.m. if other platforms make the same commitment. Some measures phase in over up to a year.

TikTok pays Alabama at least $100 million within 45 days, growing to as much as $300 million if 40 other state attorneys general sign similar agreements within a specified timeframe. The exact timeframe was not specified in the reporting reviewed. It is TikTok's first settlement with a U.S. state.

At least 27 other states and Washington, D.C., have brought similar lawsuits. The settlement mirrors safety provisions Meta agreed to in August, when it settled a sweeping state lawsuit over Instagram and Facebook for $18 billion.

The underlying claims were settled, not adjudicated. No court ruled on whether TikTok endangered children or misled consumers.
