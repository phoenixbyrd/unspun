Ford halts F-150 production at Dearborn plant for nearly a week over supply problem

All crews canceled September 24-29 over an unspecified supply problem. About 3,500 to 4,000 workers are affected.
Unspun · Listen: 3:34

Production of Ford's F-150 pickup has been halted for nearly a week at the Dearborn Truck Plant near Detroit. According to a memo viewed by Reuters, all production crews were canceled starting Thursday, September 24, through Tuesday, September 29. A person familiar with the matter said the outage was caused by a supply problem. Kansas City Assembly also canceled some shifts this week over the same issue, while the Kentucky Truck Plant near Louisville increased production to offset the losses.

The specific supplier and part are unknown. Ford declined to comment, and Reuters could not determine the cause. UAW Local 249 bargaining chair Jim Fisher suggested aluminum, but the source familiar with the matter said the outage was unrelated to the Novelis aluminum shortage that cost Ford tens of thousands of vehicles of production after fires at the supplier's New York plant in 2025.

The Detroit Free Press reported the Dearborn halt affects roughly 3,500 to 4,000 workers, and 15 shifts have been canceled at Kansas City Assembly since the beginning of September. Ford spokeswoman Christina Audisho said, "We do not comment on day-to-day operations."

The F-150 is America's best-selling vehicle, a title it has held for 44 years, according to Reuters. Ford sold 828,832 F-Series trucks last year. Ford's vehicle sales were down about 10 percent year over year through August, with F-Series sales down 11 percent. Cox Automotive projects GM and Ford will have lost more U.S. market share through the first three quarters than any other automakers, and Hyundai and Kia combined are expected to surpass Ford in third-quarter sales for the first time.

Sources: Reuters, Detroit Free Press, Cox Automotive
