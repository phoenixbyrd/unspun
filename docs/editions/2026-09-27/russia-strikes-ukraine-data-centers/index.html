Russia expands strikes on Ukrainian data centers; four killed in Kyiv

A drone strike on telecom offices in Kyiv killed four. About 100,000 households lost internet earlier in the week.
Unspun · Listen: 4:03

Russia has expanded its attacks on Ukraine's data centers and internet providers. On Friday, September 25, a Russian drone struck the Kyiv offices of Datagroup, one of Ukraine's largest telecommunications companies, killing four people and damaging a data center. In a separate attack, a 14-year-old boy was killed; Israel's Foreign Ministry identified him as an Israeli citizen.

Russian drone attacks on September 23 and 24 disrupted internet service to about 100,000 households in Kyiv and the surrounding region, according to Ukraine's Digital Ministry. The outages knocked out internet-delivered air-raid alerts civilians rely on for warnings about incoming missiles and drones. Electronic transit displays in Lutsk were also affected.

On Saturday, September 26, President Volodymyr Zelensky accused Russia of targeting civilian infrastructure to disrupt daily life, saying "all ordinary life" had become a target. Russia's Defence Ministry said its forces struck data centers it claimed were used by Ukraine's military intelligence services. Ukrainian Foreign Minister Andrii Sybiha said the facilities were civilian infrastructure essential to transmitting life-saving alerts.

Whether the struck data centers served military functions is directly disputed, and independent verification of either claim was not available in the reporting reviewed. Some companies are moving data to servers abroad. Officials warned of further outages and possible internet tariff increases from repair costs. The timeline for full restoration was not confirmed by Ukraine's Digital Ministry as of September 25.

Sources: Reuters, Ukraine's Digital Ministry, Russia's Defence Ministry, The Guardian
