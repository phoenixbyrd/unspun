U.S. and China agree to $30 billion in reciprocal tariff cuts as Xi visit ends

Non-sensitive goods are covered. Taiwan, semiconductors, and rare earths remain unresolved.
Unspun · Listen: 5:18

The Chinese president's three-day state visit to Washington ended Friday with both governments announcing an eight-point consensus. A White House fact sheet confirmed "consensus on recommendations for more favorable tariff treatment for $30 billion of non-sensitive goods in each direction."

Covered American exports include agricultural goods, wood products, medical devices, fish, and cosmetics. Covered Chinese imports include small appliances, toys, holiday decorations, and children's car seats. The cuts are reciprocal: each side reduces tariffs on $30 billion of the other's goods. The $30 billion is a fraction of total annual bilateral trade, which runs into the hundreds of billions.

The phrase "non-sensitive goods" defines the limits. Neither side touched advanced semiconductors, chipmaking equipment, rare earth minerals, or military applications, which remain under the heaviest restrictions.

On artificial intelligence, both sides agreed to a dialogue on AI risks and benefits, with the next round in November, plus a communication channel for AI-related incidents. On September 24, Xi said the U.S. and China must "ensure that the development of AI is always under human control." On September 26, Trump told reporters the two agreed to replace the term "artificial intelligence" with "superintelligence." China also agreed to buy at least 10 million metric tons of American coal annually in 2027 and 2028.

There is no specific bulk agricultural purchase commitment, only a working group on market access. Farmers had sought enforceable annual commitments on soybeans and sorghum. U.S. Trade Representative Jamieson Greer told CNBC more details would be released Monday, September 28.

Trump imposed a 10 percent tariff on Chinese goods in February; it escalated to 145 percent, China retaliated with 125 percent, and a May truce brought the U.S. rate to about 30 percent. This week the truce was extended two months past its November 10 expiration. Taiwan, semiconductors, rare earths, industrial overcapacity, and investment screening have no timelines attached.

Sources: White House fact sheet, China's Foreign Ministry, CNBC, Reuters
