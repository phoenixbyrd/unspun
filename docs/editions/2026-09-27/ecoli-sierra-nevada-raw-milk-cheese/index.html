E. coli outbreak linked to Sierra Nevada raw-milk cheese: 13 sick, 8 hospitalized

Thirteen ill across nine states; eight hospitalized, three with kidney failure. All four Graziers varieties were recalled.
Unspun · Listen: 3:27

The CDC and FDA announced September 25 that a multistate outbreak of Shiga toxin-producing E. coli O26:H11 has been linked to Graziers grass-fed raw-milk cheese, made by Sierra Nevada Cheese Company of Willows, California.

Thirteen people in nine states have fallen ill: three each in California and Nevada, and one each in Colorado, Georgia, Kentucky, Michigan, Oregon, Tennessee, and Utah. Eight were hospitalized and three developed hemolytic uremic syndrome, a type of kidney failure. No deaths were reported. More than half of the patients are children age 5 or younger; ages range from 1 to 58, with a median age of 5. All nine people interviewed about foods eaten before their illness reported eating the cheese. Illness onsets ran from July 7 to August 26.

All four Graziers raw-milk varieties were recalled September 24 after the FDA contacted the company: medium cheddar, sharp cheddar, jalapeno jack, and Monterey jack. The cheese was sold nationwide in stores and online and has a long shelf life. The FDA advises checking refrigerators and freezers and discarding any unidentifiable raw-milk cheese.

The source of the contamination and whether additional products are affected remain under investigation. Health officials caution the true case count may be higher, since many people recover without being tested. This is the fourth raw-milk cheese outbreak in about two and a half years, with 43 sick total according to the Marler Blog. No regulatory change has been announced.

Sources: CDC, FDA, NewsNation, Food Safety News
