Mortgage rates break past 7% as 10-year Treasury hits two-decade high

Freddie Mac put the 30-year fixed rate at 7.03%. The bond market behind it is at its highest in more than 20 years.
Unspun · Listen: 4:41

The average 30-year fixed mortgage rate crossed 7 percent this week for the first time since January 2025. Freddie Mac's weekly survey put the rate at 7.03 percent on September 24. The Mortgage Bankers Association reported 7.12 percent the prior week, the highest since May 2024. Daily tracker Mortgage News Daily recorded 7.37 to 7.49 percent on September 24 and 25.

The driver is the bond market. The 10-year Treasury yield closed Friday just below 5.2 percent, its highest in more than two decades, with intraday highs of 5.18 to 5.21 percent reported. Mortgage rates follow the 10-year yield.

Bright MLS chief economist Lisa Sturtevant said an increase from 6.5 to 7 percent adds more than $125 to the typical monthly payment on a median-priced U.S. home. The rate was as low as 5.99 percent at the end of February.

About half of mortgaged homeowners pay less than 4 percent and are reluctant to sell and re-borrow at 7 percent, freezing inventory.

Fed Governor Michael Barr said this week: "Economic growth is strong, and the labor market is solid. But inflation is above our 2% target and not clearly trending toward target in a timely way." The Fed raised its target range to 3.75 to 4.00 percent on September 16. CME FedWatch put the market odds of another October hike at about 70.9 percent as of September 24, a probability, not a decision. The figures come from surveys with different methodologies and are not directly comparable.

Sources: Freddie Mac, Mortgage Bankers Association, Mortgage News Daily, NPR, Bright MLS, CME FedWatch
