White House moves to cancel $810 million in approved spending days before fiscal year ends

The pocket rescission targets refugee assistance, housing counseling, education, and research funds. Its legality is unresolved.
Unspun · Listen: 6:04

The White House announced Friday night it will cancel $810 million Congress had approved, via a "pocket rescission" notification from the president to House Speaker Mike Johnson. The breakdown: $567 million for the HHS Refugee and Entrant Assistance account, $70 million for international education programs, $56 million in HUD counseling grants, $25 million in migrant student education, $28 million in HHS research grants, $10 million of $50 million for Minority Business Development Agency programs, and $9 million in foreign debt relief tied to climate policies.

The Impoundment Control Act of 1974 bars the president from simply declining to spend appropriated funds. The formal process gives Congress 45 days to approve a rescission proposal. A pocket rescission submits the proposal so late in the fiscal year that the clock runs past September 30, when the money expires. The fiscal year ends Wednesday, September 30, five days after the announcement. Once funds lapse, restoring them requires a new appropriation. Agencies cannot spend funds under a rescission proposal while Congress considers it, so the programs are effectively frozen.

The White House says the money is no longer needed because illegal border crossings have fallen, calling it spending that "does not benefit American citizens." Named in the announcement: Southwest Key Programs ($40 million), which faced a Justice Department suit alleging staff sexually abused unaccompanied minors, and Global Refuge, led by a former Obama administration policy director.

Senator Susan Collins (R), chair of Senate Appropriations, called it an effort to "undermine Congress's Constitutional power of the purse," said lawmakers got no warning, and said OMB "intentionally withheld these funds for months." Senator Patty Murray (D) called it "theft from the American people, plain and simple."

Legality is unresolved. The Government Accountability Office has previously found pocket rescissions violate the act, but GAO opinions are not court orders. The administration cites older GAO correspondence and notes the Supreme Court declined to block last year's $4.9 billion foreign-aid rescission during litigation, a case that turned partly on foreign-affairs authority inapplicable to domestic spending. No court has ruled on this package.

Sources: OMB notification, Reuters, Government Accountability Office
