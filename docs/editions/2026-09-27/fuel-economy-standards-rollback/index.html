Trump announces rollback of fuel-economy standards; formal rule expected Monday

A December proposal would cut the 2031 target from 50.4 to 34.5 mpg. The final rule text has not been released.
Unspun · Listen: 5:01

President Trump announced Saturday on Truth Social that he had "just approved new Fuel Economy Standards" that "terminate" the Biden-era electric vehicle push. Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy said the administration will formally announce the change Monday, September 28. The final regulatory text has not been released; the announcement was a social media post, not a published rule.

A December 2025 proposal from the National Highway Traffic Safety Administration would cut the fleetwide fuel-economy target from about 50.4 miles per gallon by 2031 to about 34.5 miles per gallon for cars and light trucks.

CAFE (Corporate Average Fuel Economy) dates to 1975, created after the Arab oil embargo. It sets a fleetwide average automakers must hit rather than dictating vehicle designs. The Biden administration's version effectively required a large share of electric vehicle sales, since 50.4 mpg cannot be hit on gasoline engines alone. The rollback target of about 34.5 mpg is roughly where the industry already is.

The administration projects the change will save Americans $109 billion over five years and cut the average new-vehicle price by nearly $1,000 versus the Biden standards. Trump wrote it will mean "lower prices, saving families thousands on a new, beautiful, and safe car." Congress has already zeroed out fines for automakers that miss the standards. GM, Ford, and Stellantis welcomed the change and announced billions in new U.S. manufacturing investment.

NHTSA's own modeling projects up to 100 billion more gallons of fuel burned through 2050 under the rollback. Environmental groups cite EPA analysis that eliminating the standards raises lifetime fuel costs for drivers. Both sides' numbers are projections with disputed assumptions; the direction of each effect is clear while the magnitudes are not settled. Until a final rule is published, current standards remain in effect, and the Biden-era standards were litigated for years.

Sources: Truth Social, NHTSA, EPA, Reuters
