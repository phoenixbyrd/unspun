Trump rejects Iran's proposal to pause fighting and reopen Strait of Hormuz

The proposal, conveyed through Qatari mediators, offered a seven-day pause in exchange for sanctions relief. Tehran says no definitive rejection has been conveyed.
Unspun · Listen: 6:08

President Trump said Saturday he had rejected an Iranian proposal transmitted through Qatari mediators at the United Nations General Assembly. The proposal offered a seven-day military pause plus reopening of the Strait of Hormuz in exchange for lifting American sanctions and the blockade and resuming nuclear talks. The Wall Street Journal first reported the rejection Friday, citing unnamed American officials.

"They want to make a deal, and that's fine, I like making a deal too, but that deal would not be acceptable," Trump told reporters outside the White House. He said Iran "outsmarted themselves" by closing the strait and described Iran as "dying" with "no money coming in." He also mocked Iran's missile capabilities, saying, "They had trouble with the missiles. They couldn't even shoot a missile. They were terrible at it." On Truth Social Saturday morning he wrote, in all caps, "IRAN CAN NOT HAVE A NUCLEAR WEAPON."

Iran's foreign minister, Abbas Araqchi, said the mediators had not officially conveyed a definitive American rejection to Tehran: "We are waiting for the mediators to convey the definitive views to us." Iran's president, Pezeshkian, said Tehran remains open to negotiation but distrusts Washington. The Journal also reported Trump is privately skeptical Iran would meet his demands and told staff he sees a renewed bombing campaign as likely after the November 3 midterms, sourced to unnamed officials.

The Strait of Hormuz, between Iran and Oman, is the world's most important oil chokepoint; roughly a fifth of the world's petroleum consumption passes through it. Reporting puts tanker transit roughly 90 percent below pre-war levels. Trump's statement that "massive amounts of oil are coming out of the Hormuz Strait" is not supported by the shipping data cited in coverage.

Brent crude closed Thursday at $106.60 a barrel, up 3.4 percent, and West Texas Intermediate at $94.61, up 2.7 percent. Both sold off Friday on hopes of a deal. Brent is up more than 17 percent in September. Regular gasoline in Los Angeles was above $7 a gallon, per an AFP photo caption. A Reuters/Ipsos poll from September 21 found 34 percent approve of the Iran strikes and 17 percent approve of the president's handling of the cost of living. The war began in February; a June truce collapsed within weeks after Iranian forces fired on commercial vessels.

Sources: The Wall Street Journal, Reuters, Truth Social, AFP
