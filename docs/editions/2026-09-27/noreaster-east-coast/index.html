Nor'easter batters East Coast; one dead in Brooklyn, tens of thousands without power

The storm has hit the coast from Virginia to Maine since Friday night. Warnings continue through Monday evening.
Unspun · Listen: 4:55

A powerful nor'easter has been battering the East Coast from Virginia to Maine since Friday night, bringing coastal flooding, heavy rain, and strong winds. As of Sunday morning, rain and strong winds continued across the region, with the heaviest rain expected from southern New Jersey to northern Massachusetts.

A 56-year-old man was killed in East New York, Brooklyn, on Saturday afternoon when 45-mile-per-hour gusts toppled a tree onto Euclid Avenue. He was pronounced dead at Brookdale Hospital after 5 p.m., according to police cited in the reporting. His name was not released.

Nearly 80,000 customers were without power across New Jersey, New York, Pennsylvania, Connecticut, and Massachusetts as of Saturday evening, according to the PowerOutage.com map cited by NPR. At the peak Saturday, the number exceeded 100,000. FlightAware counted 445 flights canceled and 3,016 delayed at U.S. airports as of Saturday night.

In Atlantic City, New Jersey, resident Talia Manno told NPR that waves reached halfway up the front steps of her home, the highest water she had seen since Hurricane Sandy. In Scituate, Massachusetts, waves crashed over homes on Rebecca Road. Videos showed people kayaking in flooded New York City streets. The governors of New York and New Jersey declared states of emergency Friday; Connecticut activated its Emergency Operations Center.

The National Weather Service expects the storm to meander offshore Sunday, with rain lingering over much of the Northeast and coastal flooding possible at high tides. The weakened storm is expected to sit on or just off the coast Monday, with showers and gusty winds lingering over New England, Long Island, and the Hudson Valley. The storm is expected to last through Monday evening. Coastal wind gusts could reach 60 mph. Boston is under a flood watch until 2 a.m. Tuesday.

NWS meteorologist Francis Tarasiewicz noted the storm arrived earlier than the typical October-through-May nor'easter season and is lasting longer than normal. The Weather Service advises against driving through flooded streets; its guidance states six inches of moving water can knock an adult off their feet and twelve inches can carry away a car.

Sources: National Weather Service, PowerOutage.com via NPR, FlightAware, weather.com live updates, USA Today
