FDA raises Vitruvias thyroid tablet recall to Class I, its most serious level

Lot 504950 of Vitruvias Thyroid Tablets 30 mg may be "superpotent." No injuries have been reported.
Unspun · Listen: 3:35

The Food and Drug Administration has raised a thyroid drug recall to Class I, its most serious designation, meaning there is a reasonable probability the product could cause serious health consequences or death.

On September 22, the FDA categorized the recall of one lot of Vitruvias Therapeutics' Thyroid Tablets, USP, 30 mg, as Class I. The company voluntarily recalled the lot in August after testing confirmed the tablets could be "superpotent," stronger than intended, which can cause hyperthyroidism (overactive thyroid). Symptoms include weight loss, heat intolerance, fatigue, hypertension, rapid heart rate, and heart rhythm disturbances. The elderly, pregnant women, and infants are at greatest risk.

The affected product: NDC 69680-166-00, Lot 504950, expiration September 30, 2026. Of 3,655 bottles released, 1,955 were sold, distributed nationwide from January 31 to September 30, 2025. The tablets are off-white to pale brown, round, possibly flecked, marked "THY" on one side and "050" on the other.

Vitruvias said it had received no reports of adverse events tied to the recall as of its August notice. The Class I designation reflects risk, not confirmed injuries, and only this single lot is affected.

The company noted the product is not levothyroxine or Synthroid, which are dosed in micrograms, a distinction aimed at reducing confusion. Roughly 7 percent of Americans have an active levothyroxine prescription.

Sources: FDA, USA Today, Vitruvias Therapeutics
