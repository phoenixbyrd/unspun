Meta unveils camera-free Ray-Ban glasses, Gen 3 line, and 'Muse Charm' AI device

The lineup includes camera-free Ray-Bans, a $1,299 VR device, and the palm-sized Muse Charm AI device.
Unspun · Listen: 3:49

Meta unveiled a new lineup of smart eyewear and AI hardware at its Meta Connect event September 23 and 24. The lineup includes the Ray-Ban Meta Gen 3, from $449, with a 12-megapixel camera, 3K video, slimmer frames, and up to 9 hours of battery, on sale in the U.S. since September 24; the Ray-Ban Meta Audio, from $349, camera-free, with up to 12 hours of battery, shipping October 13, the company's first glasses without a camera; the Meta Adventurer, from $249, on sale October 23; VR glasses at $1,299 targeting spring 2027; and a palm-sized AI device called Muse Charm for the holidays, with no price announced. The company said its Muse personal AI agent will come to its smart glasses in the coming months.

The launch comes amid scrutiny: 75 advocacy organizations led by the ACLU had pressed Meta over plans to add facial recognition to its eyewear. Reuters reported smart-glass sales topped 7 million units last year, making Meta the focal point of privacy lawsuits and bans at some U.K. venues. Meta has not presented privacy concerns as its reason for the camera-free model.

Meta shares closed at $734.00 on September 23, down 2.44 percent on the day.

Prices and dates for the Philippines and nine other newly announced markets (Poland, Portugal, Israel, South Africa, Indonesia, Thailand, Hong Kong, New Zealand, Malaysia) were not announced. Whether the promised Muse glasses capabilities, such as responding to what the wearer is looking at, will actually ship is unknown; they were described as forthcoming. Meta has not stated a price for Muse Charm.

Sources: USA Today, Reuters, Meta/EssilorLuxottica, Digital Watch Observatory
