Alex: The United States Senate on Monday passed the first federal overhaul of college athlete compensation rules, sending it to the House. The vote was seventy-seven to twenty-two.

Jordan: The bill is the Protect College Sports Act. It was written by Senators Maria Cantwell, Democrat of Washington, and Ted Cruz, Republican of Texas, and introduced in June. The Senate invoked cloture on September twenty-fourth by seventy-four to twenty-five, then passed the bill Monday. The official release came from the Senate Commerce Committee, with Reuters reporting the vote on Monday.

Alex: What the bill does: it creates a federal right to name, image, and likeness compensation. Schools must share a percentage of media rights, ticket sales, and sponsorships with athletes, a revenue-sharing requirement. Agent fees are capped at five percent. Scholarships are guaranteed for ten years after eligibility ends. Division One schools must cover out-of-pocket medical costs for sports-related injuries and illness for five years after eligibility ends. Schools cannot pull scholarships over injury or poor performance. Athletes may transfer once with immediate eligibility. Football coaches are barred from midseason moves. And schools must maintain a minimum number of women's and Olympic sports.
