Alex: Mortgage rates have broken past seven percent, deepening the freeze in the American housing market.

Jordan: Freddie Mac's weekly survey put the average thirty-year fixed rate at seven point zero three percent on September twenty-fourth, the first time it has crossed seven percent since January twenty twenty-five. The Mortgage Bankers Association reported seven point one two percent the prior week, the highest since May twenty twenty-four. The daily tracker Mortgage News Daily recorded seven point three seven to seven point four nine percent on September twenty-fourth and twenty-fifth.

Alex: What is driving it?

Jordan: The bond market. The ten-year Treasury yield closed Friday just below five point two percent, its highest level in more than two decades. Intraday highs of five point one eight to five point two one percent were reported. Mortgage rates follow the ten-year yield, so when bonds sell off, home loans get more expensive.

Alex: What does that cost a buyer in practice?

Jordan: Bright M L S chief economist Lisa Sturtevant said a rate increase from six point five percent to seven percent adds more than one hundred twenty-five dollars to the typical monthly payment on a median-priced American home. For context, the rate was as low as five point nine nine percent at the end of February.

Alex: Why does this freeze the market rather than just slow it?

Jordan: Because about half of mortgaged homeowners pay less than four percent. Selling now means giving up that rate and re-borrowing at seven percent, so owners stay put, and inventory stays frozen.

Alex: What is the Federal Reserve saying?

Jordan: Fed Governor Michael Barr said this week, quote, economic growth is strong, and the labor market is solid. But inflation is above our two percent target and not clearly trending toward target in a timely way, end quote. The Fed raised its target range to three point seven five to four percent on September sixteenth.

Alex: Is another hike coming?

Jordan: The market puts the odds at about seventy point nine percent for another hike in October, according to C M E FedWatch as of September twenty-fourth. That is a market probability, not a decision. The Fed has not announced it. How long elevated rates persist depends on incoming inflation data, with the P C E report due September thirtieth, and on the Iran conflict's effect on oil prices. Analysts cited in the coverage disagree on the path.

Alex: A note on the numbers themselves.

Jordan: The figures come from different surveys with different methodologies, daily versus weekly averages, so they are not directly comparable to each other. The direction, though, is consistent across all of them: up, and at multi-decade highs in the bond market behind them.

Alex: August sales numbers also landed this week.

Jordan: The National Association of Realtors reported existing-home sales fell to a seasonally adjusted annual rate of three point nine eight million in August, down two percent from July and one point two percent from a year ago. That was the first time sales dipped below four million since June twenty twenty-five, and it snapped a four-month streak of annual gains.

Alex: What did the trade group's chief economist say?

Jordan: Lawrence Yun said quote, mortgage rates and home sales move in opposite directions, so it's not surprising to see a mild dip in home buying activity due to high mortgage rates, end quote. Through the first eight months of the year, sales are still up one point six percent compared to twenty twenty-five.

Alex: What about prices?

Jordan: The national median existing-home price rose two percent year over year to four hundred thirty-four thousand one hundred dollars in July, the thirty-seventh consecutive month of annual price gains. Regionally, month-over-month sales held steady in the West but slumped in the Northeast, Midwest, and South, while annual sales declined everywhere except the South, where they remained flat.

Alex: And pending sales, which track contracts signed but not yet closed?

Jordan: Pending sales fell two point three percent in July and were down two point two percent from a year ago, the lowest level since January twenty twenty-six, with contract signings declining in all four U S regions month over month, according to the trade group.

Alex: The fifteen-year rate moved as well.

Jordan: The average fifteen-year fixed rate reached six point four two percent, up from six point two six percent the prior week and five point four nine percent a year ago, per Freddie Mac's September twenty-fourth survey. Freddie Mac chief economist Sam Khater said quote, the housing market remains supported by a solid labor market and an economy that is growing at a healthy rate, end quote.

Alex: One caveat on the survey itself.

Jordan: The survey covers conventional, conforming, fully amortizing purchase loans for borrowers who put twenty percent down and have excellent credit. Actual rates vary by borrower profile. The Mortgage Bankers Association reported purchase application volume declined week over week as buyers pulled back. And one analysis put the cost of the move at roughly one hundred ninety-three dollars more per month than a year ago on a typical loan.
