Alex: Friday night, the White House announced it will cancel eight hundred ten million dollars that Congress had already approved.

Jordan: The mechanism is called a pocket rescission, and a notification letter went from the president to House Speaker Mike Johnson. Here is the breakdown from the O M B notification: five hundred sixty-seven million dollars for the H H S Refugee and Entrant Assistance account. Seventy million for international education programs. Fifty-six million in H U D counseling grants. Twenty-five million in migrant student education. Twenty-eight million in H H S research grants. Ten million of fifty million for Minority Business Development Agency programs. And nine million in foreign debt relief tied to climate policies.

Alex: How does a pocket rescission work?

Jordan: The Impoundment Control Act of nineteen seventy-four says the president cannot simply decline to spend appropriated funds. The formal process: the president proposes a rescission, Congress has forty-five days to approve it, and if Congress does not act, the money must be spent. A pocket rescission is the maneuver of submitting the proposal so late in the fiscal year that the forty-five-day clock runs past September thirtieth, when the money expires. Congress never votes, and the funds lapse.

Alex: What is the timeline from here?

Jordan: The fiscal year ends Wednesday, September thirtieth, five days after the announcement. The forty-five-day clock runs past the expiration date either way. Once the money lapses, restoring it would require a new appropriation. In the meantime, the programs are effectively frozen. Agencies cannot spend funds that are under a rescission proposal while Congress considers it.

Alex: What is the administration's stated rationale?

Jordan: The White House says the money is no longer needed because illegal border crossings have fallen, and called it spending that quote does not benefit American citizens, end quote.

Alex: Who specifically loses funding?

Jordan: Named in the announcement: Southwest Key Programs, forty million dollars. The White House notes the organization faced a Justice Department suit alleging staff sexually abused unaccompanied minors. Also Global Refuge, which is led by a former Obama administration policy director.

Alex: And the objections?

Jordan: Republican Senator Susan Collins, who chairs the Senate Appropriations Committee, called it an effort to quote undermine Congress's Constitutional power of the purse, end quote. She said lawmakers got no warning and that O M B quote intentionally withheld these funds for months, end quote. Democratic Senator Patty Murray called it quote theft from the American people, plain and simple, end quote.

Alex: Is it legal?

Jordan: Genuinely unresolved. The Government Accountability Office, Congress's watchdog, has previously found that pocket rescissions violate the Impoundment Control Act. G A O opinions are not court orders. They carry weight with Congress and judges, but they do not decide cases. The administration points to older G A O correspondence and notes that the Supreme Court declined to block last year's four point nine billion dollar foreign-aid rescission while litigation continued. But that case turned partly on foreign-affairs authority, which does not apply here, because this package targets domestic spending. No court has ruled on this package. Anyone stating it is definitively legal or definitively illegal is overstating what is known.

Alex: What is affected?

Jordan: Programs serving refugees, unaccompanied minors, homebuyers who use H U D counseling, and minority-owned businesses. And beyond the dollars, a dispute over who controls federal spending: the Congress that appropriated it, or the president declining to spend it.

Alex: When exactly did this arrive, and what are the calendar constraints?

Jordan: The White House notified Congress on Friday evening, September twenty-fifth, covering eleven spending items. The fiscal year ends Wednesday, September thirtieth. The Senate is scheduled to be in session from September twenty-eighth through the thirtieth. The House, however, is out of session through the November election, which leaves lawmakers little room to respond, according to Fox News. Separately, a bipartisan continuing resolution passed on September twenty-fourth set a new funding deadline of December eleventh, twenty twenty-six, but it did not restrict unilateral spending cuts.

Alex: What is the administration's legal position?

Jordan: Office of Management and Budget Director Russ Vought has maintained that the Impoundment Control Act does not bar a president from canceling funds late in the fiscal year, according to Fox News. The Congressional Research Service summarized the administration's argument that the Act does not explicitly forbid rescissions near the end of a fiscal year. But the same C R S summary notes the G A O has likened pocket rescissions to a line-item veto, a power the Supreme Court ruled unconstitutional.

Alex: What history exists for this maneuver?

Jordan: Last year's four point nine billion dollar foreign-aid cancellation was the first pocket rescission in nearly five decades, according to C B S News. The Supreme Court declined to block it while declining to rule on its legality, A B C News reported. The last pocket rescission before that took place under President Jimmy Carter, and he proposed it well before the forty-five-day congressional review deadline.

Alex: What else did the White House cite as it announced the cuts?

Jordan: The announcement said the funding had supported bringing foreign students and faculty to the United States and, in some cases, doctoral dissertations on quote queer and trans community building in foreign countries, end quote. It also targeted a Justice Department office focused on reducing racial tensions, and described several of the grants as quote outright harmful and blatantly ideological, end quote. It noted that several of the organizations are led by former officials in President Barack Obama's administration.

Alex: More reaction from Capitol Hill.

Jordan: Senate Minority Leader Chuck Schumer called it an illegal stripping of nearly one billion dollars in critical funding. Senator Jeff Merkley of Oregon said Trump and Vought quote violated the law by sending Congress more illegal pocket rescissions in the dark of night, end quote. Representative Rosa DeLauro of Connecticut, the top Democrat on the House Appropriations Committee, accused the administration of stealing money owed to communities, according to C B S News.
