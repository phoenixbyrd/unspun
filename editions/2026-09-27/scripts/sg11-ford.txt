Alex: Production of Ford's F-150 pickup has been halted for nearly a week at the Dearborn Truck Plant near Detroit.

Jordan: According to a memo viewed by Reuters, all production crews were canceled starting Thursday, September twenty-fourth, through Tuesday, September twenty-ninth. A person familiar with the matter said the outage was caused by a supply problem. Kansas City Assembly also canceled some shifts this week over the same issue, while the Kentucky Truck Plant near Louisville increased production to offset the losses.

Alex: What is the supply problem?

Jordan: That is unknown. Ford declined to comment, and Reuters could not determine the specific supplier or part. U A W Local two forty-nine bargaining chair Jim Fisher suggested aluminum, but the source familiar with the matter said the outage was unrelated to the Novelis aluminum shortage that cost Ford tens of thousands of vehicles of production after fires at the supplier's New York plant in twenty twenty-five. The cause remains unverified.

Alex: How many workers are affected?

Jordan: The Detroit Free Press reported the Dearborn halt affects roughly three thousand five hundred to four thousand workers. Fifteen shifts have been canceled at Kansas City Assembly since the beginning of September. A Ford spokeswoman, Christina Audisho, said quote, we do not comment on day-to-day operations, end quote.

Alex: How significant is the F-150?

Jordan: It is America's best-selling vehicle, a title it has held for forty-four years, according to Reuters. Ford sold eight hundred twenty-eight thousand eight hundred thirty-two F-Series trucks last year.

Alex: What is the broader picture for Ford?

Jordan: Ford's vehicle sales were down about ten percent year over year through August, with F-Series sales down eleven percent. Cox Automotive projects G M and Ford will have lost more American market share through the first three quarters than any other automakers, and Hyundai and Kia combined are expected to surpass Ford in third-quarter sales for the first time.

Alex: The scale of the Dearborn stoppage is clearer now.

Jordan: Ford lists approximately four thousand nine hundred ninety-five employees at Dearborn Truck, including about four thousand six hundred sixty-four hourly workers as of June twenty twenty-six. U A W Region One A director Mark DePaoli told the Detroit Free Press that roughly three thousand five hundred to four thousand workers had been told to stay home during the stoppage. The plant is part of the Rouge manufacturing complex in Michigan and builds the F-150 and the high-performance F-150 Raptor.

Alex: Is the return date firm?

Jordan: The memo cited by Reuters scheduled crews through Tuesday, September twenty-ninth. A union official separately told the Detroit Free Press that production was expected to remain halted through September thirtieth, suggesting the precise return date depends on shift scheduling and resolution of the supplier problem.

Alex: What did Reuters say about the cost?

Jordan: Reuters noted that losing even a single day of production is extremely expensive for the automaker, especially for the F-Series, America's best-selling vehicle for forty-four years. Carscoops separately reported the F-Series has been America's best-selling truck for forty-nine years.

Alex: How does this fit with the aluminum shortage earlier this year?

Jordan: Ford spent much of twenty twenty-six recovering from earlier aluminum shortages that constrained F-Series production, and inventories were still rebuilding when the latest stoppage emerged. Novelis, the supplier whose plant fires caused that shortage, fired up the hot mill at its Oswego plant in June and said it was quote working closely with customers to ramp up supply, end quote.

Alex: And Kansas City?

Jordan: Kansas City Assembly, which builds F-150s alongside the Transit van, canceled some shifts over the same supplier issue. As of Friday, no dealer shortage had been reported, but both of Ford's main U S F-150 assembly locations were affected at once.
