Alex: The Food and Drug Administration has raised a thyroid drug recall to Class One, its most serious designation.

Jordan: On September twenty-second, the F D A categorized the recall of one lot of Vitruvias Therapeutics' Thyroid Tablets, U S P, thirty milligrams, as Class One. That means there is a reasonable probability the product could cause serious health consequences or death. The company voluntarily recalled the lot in August after testing confirmed the tablets could be quote superpotent, end quote, stronger than intended, which can cause hyperthyroidism, an overactive thyroid. Symptoms include weight loss, heat intolerance, fatigue, hypertension, rapid heart rate, and heart rhythm disturbances. The elderly, pregnant women, and infants are at greatest risk.

Alex: Which product exactly?

Jordan: N D C six nine six eight zero, one six six, zero zero. Lot five zero four nine five zero. Expiration September thirtieth, twenty twenty-six. Three thousand six hundred fifty-five bottles were released and one thousand nine hundred fifty-five sold, distributed nationwide from January thirty-first to September thirtieth, twenty twenty-five. The tablets are off-white to pale brown, round, possibly flecked, marked T H Y on one side and zero five zero on the other.

Alex: Have there been injuries?

Jordan: The company said it had received no reports of adverse events tied to the recall as of its August notice. The Class One designation reflects risk, not confirmed injuries. Only this single lot is affected.

Alex: Could this be confused with other thyroid medications?

Jordan: The company noted the product is not levothyroxine or Synthroid, which are dosed in micrograms, a distinction aimed at reducing confusion. Roughly seven percent of Americans have an active levothyroxine prescription.

Alex: What exactly is in these tablets?

Jordan: The product contains levothyroxine, known as T four, and liothyronine, known as T three. It is a natural thyroid preparation derived from porcine thyroid glands, used to treat hypothyroidism, goiters, and thyroid cancer. That is distinct from synthetic levothyroxine products like Synthroid.

Alex: Walk through the timeline.

Jordan: Vitruvias Therapeutics, based in Auburn, Alabama, initiated the recall on August twenty-first, twenty twenty-six, and sent a public notice by letter that day. The F D A published the original recall notice August twenty-fourth. The company said it had received no reports of adverse events tied to the recall as of that notice's publication. On September twenty-second, the F D A elevated it to Class One, and the F D A enforcement report listed it September twenty-third.

Alex: What should pharmacies do?

Jordan: The recall guidance for pharmacists says to identify and quarantine lot five zero four nine five zero, remove it from inventory, and coordinate with prescribers to provide an alternative supply. The lot was distributed nationwide through Vitruvias Therapeutics' direct accounts.

Alex: And the company can be contacted directly?

Jordan: Vitruvias listed an email address, safety at vitruvias dot com, and a phone number, two five six, two three nine, nine three seven three, for questions. USA Today reported about one thousand nine hundred units were sold from a batch of about three thousand six hundred.

Alex: What did the reporting say about the risk?

Jordan: The reporting cited concerns that taking too much thyroid hormone can include fatigue, undesired weight loss, and rapid heart rate or heart arrhythmias. Anyone taking the recalled tablets is advised to contact a health care provider for guidance, according to the F D A notice. Only this single lot is affected. It is not a recall of every thyroid medication, every levothyroxine product, or every product containing thyroid hormone.
