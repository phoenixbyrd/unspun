Alex: The Chinese president's three-day state visit to Washington wrapped up Friday, and both governments announced what they agreed to.

Jordan: China's foreign ministry announced Saturday an eight-point consensus, and a White House fact sheet released Friday night confirmed quote consensus on recommendations for more favorable tariff treatment for thirty billion dollars of non-sensitive goods in each direction, end quote.

Alex: Which goods are covered?

Jordan: On the American export side: agricultural goods, wood products, medical devices, fish, and cosmetics. On the Chinese import side: small appliances, toys, holiday decorations, and children's car seats. The word reciprocal matters here. Each side is cutting tariffs on thirty billion dollars of the other's goods.

Alex: Put thirty billion in perspective.

Jordan: It is real money, but a fraction of total annual trade between the two countries, which runs into the hundreds of billions. And the phrase quote non-sensitive goods end quote defines the limits. Neither side touched advanced semiconductors, the equipment that makes them, rare earth minerals, or anything with a military application. Those remain under the heaviest restrictions.

Alex: What does it mean for shoppers?

Jordan: In the near term, it should modestly lower prices, or at least stop them rising, on the covered goods. These are products where tariffs show up directly at the register, because importers pass the cost through. The timing matters: the covered goods include holiday-season items like toys and decorations. U S Trade Representative Jamieson Greer told C N B C Friday that more details would be released Monday, September twenty-eighth, including which product categories are covered and when the cuts take effect.

Alex: What else came out of the visit?

Jordan: On artificial intelligence, both sides agreed to a dialogue on A I risks and benefits, with the next round set for November, plus a communication channel for A I related incidents. On September twenty-fourth, Xi said the United States and China must quote ensure that the development of A I is always under human control, end quote. On September twenty-sixth, Trump told reporters the two agreed to replace the term quote artificial intelligence end quote with quote superintelligence, end quote. Separately, China agreed to buy at least ten million metric tons of American coal annually in twenty twenty-seven and twenty twenty-eight.

Alex: What did American farmers get?

Jordan: Less than they wanted. There is no specific bulk agricultural purchase commitment, only a working group on agriculture market access. Farmers had sought enforceable annual purchase commitments, especially on soybeans and sorghum, after China diversified toward Brazil. A working group is not a contract. Whether Monday's details include anything firmer is the thing to watch.

Alex: The background on the tariffs.

Jordan: Trump imposed a ten percent tariff on Chinese goods in February. It escalated to one hundred forty-five percent. China retaliated with one hundred twenty-five percent. A truce in May brought the American rate to about thirty percent, and this week the two sides extended that truce two months past its November tenth expiration. The extension keeps the heavier tariffs frozen. It does not lower them.

Alex: And what remains unresolved?

Jordan: Taiwan, semiconductors, rare earths, industrial overcapacity, and investment screening. None of them have timelines attached.

Alex: More on the visit itself and what made it notable.

Jordan: Xi arrived at Joint Base Andrews in Maryland on Wednesday evening for a three-day state visit, his first trip to the United States capital in about a decade. President Trump and First Lady Melania Trump personally greeted Xi and his wife, Peng Liyuan, on the tarmac. Xi said in written remarks after arriving that China and the United States should be quote partners rather than rivals, end quote. The summit ended Friday without a major public breakthrough, and Xi returned to Beijing, according to China's state news agency Xinhua.

Alex: The tariff truce extension came before the meetings even began.

Jordan: Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent announced Wednesday that Washington and Beijing had extended their trade truce two months, to January tenth, twenty twenty-seven. Bessent had met China's Vice Premier He Lifeng in Washington the same day. The truce, known as the Busan Agreement, was reached when Trump and Xi met in Busan, South Korea, in October twenty twenty-five, and had been due to expire November tenth. This is the second Trump-Xi summit in less than six months, following Trump's visit to Beijing in May.

Alex: What else was in the eight-point consensus beyond tariffs?

Jordan: Both sides agreed to establish a standing trade council, giving Washington and Beijing a channel for resolving friction between summits, and to implement outcomes from their earlier Kuala Lumpur consultation. China will host the A P E C Economic Leaders' Meeting this November, and the United States will host the G twenty Leaders' Summit in December, and each leader said he would attend the other's event.

Alex: And on Iran?

Jordan: The two sides agreed that Tehran should fulfill its commitment not to develop nuclear weapons, and said no country or institution shall charge transit fees for passage through international waterways. Additional pledges reported in the coverage touched on stabilizing global petroleum supplies and improving military crisis communication between the two powers.

Alex: What did the fact sheets leave out?

Jordan: Neither side disclosed the proposed tariff rates, the size of the reductions, or a timeline for implementation. China's official account used stronger language than the White House fact sheet, describing an actual arrangement rather than recommendations.
